# Appendix A: The Cognition Ledger on One Page

The Cognition Ledger, the AI Value Stream Mapping method, is a management system with one job: turning the time that AI gives back into money a chief financial officer can find on the P&L. It treats every automation idea like a ticket in a register, measures it on time, money and quality, and refuses to call anything savings until finance has verified it. Its core line is simple: **hours aren't money until they're banked.**

This page is the whole system in one view. Tape it next to wherever your team tracks its AI work.

## The Six Moves

Every idea moves through six stages in order. Each ends at a gate, and a ticket can't move on until it passes.

| Move | The question it answers | What it produces | The gate to move on |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Map | Where is the thinking work, and where is it wasted? | A cognitive value stream map with touch time, wait time and %C&A for each step | Waste deleted or simplified before anyone automates it |
| 2. Sort | Should this be plain workflow automation, a Skill, an agent, or stay with a person? | A Sort Score and a recommended type | All nine scores entered; the business owner agrees the type |
| 3. Size | How much time, money and quality does it change? | A measured baseline and a projection for all three | Baseline measured, not guessed, on at least 20 real units |
| 4. Ship | Does it work on real cases? | A built and piloted Skill or agent | New minutes per unit and new error rate measured in the pilot |
| 5. Bank | Where does every freed hour go? | An Hour Budget signed by the budget owner | Every hour has a lever or is shown as Leak |
| 6. Prove | Did the money show up? | CFO-verified realized value by quarter | Finance has seen the evidence for each line |

**The override gate:** nothing enters Building without a draft Hour Budget. If nobody can say where the hours will go before you build, they will leak after you ship.

## The Statuses and What It Takes to Enter Each

The ledger runs like an issue tracker. Its columns are the statuses, and each has an entry rule.

| Status | It means | Entry rule |
|---|---|---|
| Intake | The idea is logged on an intake card | A named unit of work, trigger and output |
| Sorted | Skill, agent, workflow automation or keep human has been decided | Nine Sort Score answers |
| Sized | Time, money and quality projected | A baseline measured on at least 20 real units |
| Building | Being built and tested on real cases | A draft Hour Budget |
| Live | Running in production; actuals being measured | Pilot minutes and error rate in the ledger |
| Banked | Every freed hour has a destination | The budget owner's signature |
| Realized | Money verified on a P&L line | Finance's verification, with a date |
| Parked | Not now | A reason recorded in the notes |

Keep a work-in-progress limit on Building: no more than three to five items per value stream at once. Starting ten builds and finishing none is the commonest way these programs fail.

## The Four Kinds of Work

| Type | Today's form | Started by | Use it when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Workflow automation | Scripts, integrations, robotic process automation | A rule or event | Rules on clean data; no judgment needed |
| Skill (packaged expertise) | A folder of instructions, templates and scripts an AI loads when a task matches | A person, who also reviews the output | Judgment inside one task, done the house way |
| Agent (autonomous cognition) | An AI system that chooses its own steps and tools toward a goal | An event, a schedule or a person | Judgment across many steps and systems, inside limits, with stop conditions |
| Keep human, AI assists | A person decides; AI researches and drafts | A person | A wrong output is severe and can't be undone |

Use the simplest one that works. Add a human checkpoint to an agent, or a second review to a Skill, when the risk calls for it. Appendix B gives the full scoring rule.

## The Six Levers

Every freed hour goes to one lever, or it is Leak, valued at \$0.

**Cut** stops paying for something (overtime, temps, contractors). **Avoid** absorbs growth or attrition without a planned hire. **Grow** moves hours to revenue work. **Fix** reduces errors, credits and write-offs. **Speed** shortens lead time so cash arrives sooner. **Invest** moves hours to a funded strategic project. Appendix C gives the P&L line, evidence and pricing for each.

## The Three Numbers

The ledger always shows three numbers side by side and never adds them together.

1. **Theoretical value**: net hours freed times the loaded hourly rate. The size of the opportunity. Never reported as savings.
2. **Projected banked value**: value with a named lever, a P&L line, a budget owner and a date.
3. **Realized value**: what the CFO, or a finance partner the CFO names, has verified against evidence. The only savings number.

Two ratios turn them into signals. **Realization rate** is realized divided by projected banked; below 50% a year after go-live means the Hour Budget isn't being worked. **Realization gap** is theoretical minus realized: the promise that hasn't reached the P&L yet.

## The Rhythm

**Weekly**, the value stream owner reviews the board and moves tickets. **Monthly**, the leak report goes to the executive sponsor. **Quarterly**, finance verifies realized lines and the CFO dashboard goes to the executive team.

## The Ten Principles

1. Decisions turn time into money.
2. See the work before you speed it.
3. Earn your way to autonomy.
4. Measure the work, not the memory.
5. The reviewer's minutes count.
6. A possibility is not a payment.
7. Every hour gets a job, or a red flag.
8. Whoever's budget moves signs first.
9. Finance holds the pen.
10. Three numbers, side by side, always.
