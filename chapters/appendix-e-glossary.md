# Appendix E: Glossary

:::{figure} ../images/appE-infographic.png
:label: fig-appE-infographic
:alt: Opening infographic summarising Glossary
:width: 80%
:align: center

*Every term in the book, in plain words.*
:::


Every term the Cognition Ledger uses, in alphabetical order. Where a term has a lasting name and a name of the moment, the lasting name comes first and today's name follows, because the tools will be renamed long before the ideas change.

**Activity ratio.** Total touch time divided by total lead time. In office work it is often 1% to 10%, meaning work sits idle 90% to 99% of the time.

**Agent.** Today's name for autonomous cognition: an AI system that directs its own steps and tool use to reach a goal, often started by an event rather than a person. It picks up a whole job, works across systems, and either finishes or hands a prepared decision to a person. "A digital worker for a whole job." Compare *Workflow*.

**Agentic system.** The umbrella term for systems in which AI models and tools work together, covering both workflows (fixed paths) and agents (paths the model chooses). The ledger's Agent type covers both, and builds the fixed-path version first whenever it does the job.

**AI Value Office.** The small team that runs the ledger across a company: it keeps the entry rules, enforces work-in-progress limits, runs the weekly, monthly and quarterly rhythm, makes kill and park decisions, and works with the finance partner who verifies realized value.

**AI Value Stream Mapping.** The business-language name for the Cognition Ledger's approach: value stream mapping retooled for an age when AI can do thinking work, with every freed hour followed to the P&L. Its method is Cognitive Value Stream Mapping (CVSM).

**Autonomous cognition.** Thinking work done by a system that chooses its own steps toward a goal, within limits, usually started by an event. Today its main form is the AI agent.

**Autonomy Index.** The average of the four autonomy scores (A1 to A4) in the Sort Score: what starts the work, how fixed the path is, how many systems it touches, and whether it must run unattended. At 3.0 or higher, the recommendation is an agent.

**Avoid.** The lever that absorbs growth or attrition without a hire the plan called for. Lands on personnel expense against budget; priced at the loaded rate of the role not hired; proved by a requisition removed from the approved headcount plan.

**Bank (move).** The fifth move: giving every freed hour a destination in a signed Hour Budget.

**Banked (status).** A ledger status meaning every freed hour has a lever or is shown as Leak, and the budget owner has signed.

**Banking rules.** The six rules of the Hour Budget: decide before you build; the budget owner signs; one hour, one destination; name the date; price it like finance would; show the leak.

**Baseline.** The measured performance of the work before change: minutes per unit, error rate, lead time. Timed on at least 20 real units, never estimated.

**Budget owner.** The person whose P&L or budget line changes when the hours are banked. They sign the Hour Budget, not the project team.

**Build cost.** The one-time cost to design, build, test and pilot a Skill, agent or automation.

**Building (status).** A ledger status meaning the idea is being built and tested on real cases. Nothing enters it without a draft Hour Budget.

**Cognition Index.** The average of the three cognition scores (C1 to C3): how much judgment the work needs, how unstructured its inputs are, and how much cases differ. Below 2.0, the work doesn't need AI.

**Cognitive value stream.** The thinking work inside a value stream: reading, deciding, writing, checking and matching. This is the part AI can now do.

**Cognitive Value Stream Mapping (CVSM).** The method at the heart of the Cognition Ledger: map the thinking work in a value stream, delete its waste, sort each step between plain automation, packaged expertise, autonomous cognition and people, size the change in time, money and quality, and bank and prove every freed hour. Described in business terms as AI Value Stream Mapping.

**Cognitive wastes.** The eight forms waste takes in knowledge work: re-keying, searching, waiting for decisions, handoffs, rework from incomplete inputs, over-processing, context switching and unused judgment.

**Compounding error.** The way small error rates multiply across steps. Five steps that are each right 95% of the time get a unit through cleanly only 77% of the time.

**Confidence factor.** A discount applied in the priority score: High (0.9) when the baseline is measured and something similar already works, Medium (0.7) when the baseline is sampled, Low (0.5) when it is estimated.

**Cost avoidance.** The value class for the Avoid lever: money the company would have spent and now won't, shown against budget rather than against last year.

**Cost of capital.** What money costs the company, either its borrowing rate or the return it expects on its investments. Used to price the Speed lever. Brightwater uses 7%.

**Cost of poor quality (COPQ).** What errors cost to find and fix, including credits, write-offs, penalties and rework time. COPQ avoided = units per year × (baseline error rate − new error rate) × cost per error.

**Cost per error.** What one error costs once it lands downstream, including rework time, credits, margin given away and penalties.

**Cut.** The lever that stops paying for something: overtime, temps, contractors, an outsourced service. Lands on operating expense; priced at the rate of what you stop paying for; proved by a canceled contract or purchase order.

**Days sales outstanding (DSO).** The average number of days customers take to pay after they are invoiced. Every day removed frees cash equal to a day of revenue.

**Error rate.** The share of units that need correction downstream. Measured where the work lands, not where it is done.

**Fix.** The lever that banks fewer errors, credits, write-offs and penalties. Lands on cost of goods sold, credits and allowances, bad debt or penalties; priced at COPQ avoided; proved by an error log or write-off report against the baseline.

**Full-time equivalent (FTE).** One person's productive year: 1,880 hours by the ledger's default.

**Gate.** The test a ticket must pass before it moves to the next move or status, such as a measured baseline before Sized or a signed Hour Budget before Banked.

**Grow.** The lever that moves hours to revenue work: selling, retention, new offers. Lands on revenue and gross margin; priced at gross margin per redeployed hour; proved by attribution against a control group.

**Hard savings.** The value class for Cut and Fix: money that stops leaving the company and can be seen against last year's spending.

**Hour Budget.** The plan that gives every freed hour a destination: a lever, a P&L line, a budget owner and a date, with any remainder shown as Leak.

**Human checkpoint.** The point at which a person approves an agent's work before it reaches a customer, a ledger or a system of record. Added when the Risk Index is 3.0 or higher.

**Intake (status).** The first ledger status: an idea logged with its unit of work, trigger and output, not yet scored.

**Invest.** The lever that sends hours to a funded strategic project with its own business case and owner. Its value is booked in that project's case, not in the ledger.

**Keep human, AI assists.** The Sort Score result when the Risk Index is 4.5 or higher: AI researches and drafts, a person decides and acts.

**Lead time.** Elapsed time from request to completion. Lead time = touch time + wait time.

**Leak.** Freed hours with no lever. Valued at \$0 on the P&L, because nothing on the books changes, and reported every quarter until assigned.

**Lever.** One of six destinations for freed hours: Cut, Avoid, Grow, Fix, Speed or Invest.

**Little's law.** A rule from queueing theory: the average number of items in a system equals their average arrival rate times the average time each spends in it. It means that, at a steady arrival rate, the only way to shorten lead time is to hold less work in progress.

**Live (status).** A ledger status meaning the Skill, agent or automation is running in production and its actual results are being measured.

**Load factor.** The share added to base pay for benefits, payroll taxes, space and equipment. The ledger's default is 30%.

**Loaded hourly rate.** What an hour of a person's time costs the company: base pay × (1 + load factor) ÷ productive hours per year.

**Map (move).** The first move: drawing the cognitive value stream with touch time, wait time and %C&A for each step, and deleting waste before anything is automated.

**Net annual banked value.** Projected banked value minus annual run cost.

**Net hours freed.** The hours an automation gives back after counting the time people spend reviewing its output and overseeing it: ((baseline minutes − new minutes) ÷ 60 × units per week − oversight hours per week) × working weeks.

**Oversight hours.** Weekly time people spend monitoring an automation, handling its exceptions and maintaining what it reads. Subtracted from hours freed.

**Packaged expertise.** Know-how written down once so that AI can apply it the house way every time it is asked: instructions, examples, templates and scripts kept together. A person starts it and a person reviews the result. Today its main form is the Skill. "Your best person's know-how, written down once and used by everyone."

**Parked (status).** A ledger status for ideas the team has decided not to pursue now, with the reason recorded.

**Payback.** Months for an automation to repay its build cost: build cost ÷ (net annual banked value ÷ 12).

**Percent complete and accurate (%C&A).** The share of units a step passes on that the next step can use as is, without correcting, adding or clarifying.

**Priority score.** Projected banked value × confidence factor ÷ effort in weeks. It ranks banked value, not theoretical value.

**Productive hours.** Hours a person is available to work in a year: 2,080 paid hours less about 200 for holidays, vacation and sick time, so 1,880 by default.

**Progressive disclosure.** The design that lets an AI hold many Skills without slowing down: it always knows each Skill's short name and description, reads the full instructions only when a task matches, and opens reference files or runs scripts only when they are needed.

**Projected banked value.** Value with a named lever, a P&L line, a budget owner and a date. The plan, not the proof.

**Prove (move).** The sixth move: finance verifying, quarter by quarter, that the banked value reached the P&L.

**Pure waste.** Activity the customer wouldn't notice if it stopped. Deleted before anything is automated.

**Realization gap.** Theoretical value minus realized value: the size of the promise that hasn't reached the P&L.

**Realization rate.** Realized value divided by projected banked value. Below 50% a year after go-live means the Hour Budget isn't being worked.

**Realized (status).** The final ledger status: finance has verified money on a P&L line or a removed budget line.

**Realized value.** Money the CFO, or a finance partner the CFO names, has verified against evidence. The only number that counts as savings.

**Revenue attributable.** The value class for the Grow lever: revenue or margin linked to redeployed hours by attribution against a control group.

**Risk Index.** The average of the two risk scores in the Sort Score: what a wrong output costs (R1) and whether it can be undone (R2). At 4.5 or higher, keep a person in charge; at 3.0 or higher, add a checkpoint or second review.

**Rolled %C&A.** All the %C&A values in a stream multiplied together: how often a unit gets through the whole stream with no rework.

**Run cost.** The annual cost of operating an automation: licences, usage, monitoring and support.

**Second review.** A second person checking a Skill's output before it is used. Added when the Risk Index is 3.0 or higher.

**Ship (move).** The fourth move: building and piloting a Skill or agent on real cases and measuring its new minutes and error rate.

**Size (move).** The third move: measuring the baseline and projecting the change in time, money and quality.

**Sized (status).** A ledger status meaning the baseline is measured and time, money and quality are projected.

**Skill.** Today's name for packaged expertise: a folder centred on a file called SKILL.md, with a short name and description an AI always sees, instructions it reads only when a task matches, and optional reference files, templates and scripts it opens only when needed. A person starts it and reviews the result. Use only Skills from sources you trust, because a Skill can tell the AI to run code.

**Sort (move).** The second move: deciding whether work goes to workflow automation, a Skill, an agent, or stays with a person.

**Sort Score.** Nine questions scored 1 to 5, averaged into the Cognition, Autonomy and Risk indexes, then run through a fixed decision rule. See Appendix B.

**Sorted (status).** A ledger status meaning the nine Sort Score answers are in and the type is agreed.

**Speed.** The lever that turns shorter lead time into cash or capacity sooner. Lands on interest expense and working capital; priced at cash freed × cost of capital, never at the loaded rate; proved by a DSO or lead-time report.

**Strategic.** The value class for the Invest lever, whose value is carried in another project's business case.

**Theoretical value.** Net hours freed × loaded hourly rate. The size of the opportunity. Never reported as savings.

**Touch time.** Minutes a person spends actively working on one unit, uninterrupted. Lean practice also calls it processing time.

**Unit.** One instance of the work: one quote, one purchase order, one dispute.

**Unused judgment.** The eighth cognitive waste: experts spending their week on clerical work. Freeing it is often the biggest prize.

**Value class.** The kind of money a lever produces: hard savings, cost avoidance, revenue attributable, cash or strategic.

**Value stream.** Every step needed to fulfil a customer request, from the request to delivery and payment. Order-to-Cash and Procure-to-Pay are value streams.

**Value stream mapping (VSM).** The lean practice, from the Toyota Production System, of following one product or service from request to delivery and drawing every step, wait, handoff and rework loop so waste becomes visible.

**Value stream owner.** The leader accountable for a whole value stream end to end, who runs its ledger board.

**Value-adding.** Activity a customer would pay for. Compare *necessary non-value-adding* (required today by regulation, control or technology) and *pure waste*.

**Wait time.** Time a unit sits between steps: in a queue, an inbox or someone's head.

**WIP limit.** A cap on work in progress, here the number of ideas allowed in Building at once, typically three to five per value stream. Fewer things in progress means each finishes sooner.

**Workflow.** In AI systems, a design where models and tools follow predefined code paths. Compare *Agent*, where the model chooses the path.

**Workflow automation.** Fixed, rule-based automation such as scripts, integrations and robotic process automation, with no AI judgment. The right answer when the Cognition Index is below 2.0.

**Working weeks.** Weeks a person works in a year: 47 by the ledger's default.
